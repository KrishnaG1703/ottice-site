how it works

three moments. that's the whole job.

  1. 01

    a secret appears.

    a password field, an api-key input, or a one-time key shown as page text. otter notices, and saves nothing yet.

  2. 02

    otter asks.

    no background hoarding, no vague consent. you decide whether this exact value belongs in the vault.

  3. 03

    it fills the right site. only that one.

    otter checks the exact protocol, hostname and port. a lookalike like examp1e.dev gets nothing.

security, honestly

friendly on the outside. careful underneath.

here's what exists in the browser extension today, and what doesn't yet.

built

authenticated encryption

aes-256-gcm protects the whole entry, label, site and username included. the key comes from your passphrase through 600,000 rounds of pbkdf2.

built

strict site checks

every save and fill is matched to the page the browser itself says sent it. a page can't claim to be somewhere it isn't.

built

click-hijack protection

otter only acts on a real click on a prompt nothing is covering. we attack-tested this in-house with decoy overlays and lookalike domains.

next

independent audit and hardware keys

an outside security audit, then secure enclave and tpm-backed keys. on the roadmap, not claims about today.

this is a development preview.

it hasn't had an independent security audit yet. don't store cryptocurrency seeds, production api keys or valuable credentials.

questions

the practical bits.

where are my secrets stored?

only in your browser, on your device. entries are encrypted before they're written to the extension's local storage. there are no otter servers, accounts or sync, so nothing leaves your machine.

which browsers does it work in?

chrome first, and chrome-based browsers like brave, arc and vivaldi through the chrome web store. edge and firefox are next; safari comes later.

what if i forget my passphrase?

the vault can't be opened, by you or by us. there is no recovery key in this preview, which is part of why it isn't meant for important credentials yet.

what can't it do yet?

it saves logins and api keys only, with no sync, import, export or sharing. it hasn't had an independent audit. and like all autofill, a filled value lives in that site's page, so a site with a script-injection bug could read what you fill there.

does otter see everything i type?

otter looks at pages locally to spot password fields and newly revealed keys. nothing is recorded or sent anywhere, and nothing is saved unless you tap “keep it safe”.
